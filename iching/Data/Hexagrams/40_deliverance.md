# Hexagram 40 — ䷧ 解 (xiè) — Deliverance

**Alternate Names:** "Deliverance", "Taking-Apart", "Relief", "Liberation", "Dissolution"

**Upper Trigram:** ☳ 震 (zhèn) — Thunder (雷)
**Lower Trigram:** ☵ 坎 (kǎn) — Water (水)

**King Wen Number:** 40 | **Binary:** 001010

---

## Overview

Deliverance signifies the dissolution of tension, the loosening of bonds, and the restoration of free movement after a period of confinement. Like the breaking of an impasse, this hexagram marks the transition from difficulty toward renewed possibility. Benefit comes not merely from relief but from taking wise action during the window of opportunity that liberation creates.

## Judgment

Deliverance is auspicious when followed by purposeful action toward advantageous destinations. The moment of release creates an opening; the superior person must direct this newfound freedom toward genuine benefit. Relief itself is incomplete without the wisdom to move toward what matters.

## Image

Thunder breaks forth from water, shattering constraint and releasing accumulated force in active motion. This symbolizes sudden liberation and the energetic movement following release from obstruction.

## The Lines

### Six at the Beginning

No action required; deliverance proceeds naturally from the dissolution of constraint. The release of tension requires no force; like a knot naturally loosening, the obstruction simply falls away. This phase brings benefit through effortless passage.

### Nine in the Second Place

Three foxes captured in the field bring caution about incomplete deliverance and hidden dangers. While the primary obstacle dissolves, secondary difficulties may remain unaddressed. The superior person remains vigilant even amid relief, seeking to eliminate remaining complications.

### Six in the Third Place

Bearing a heavy load while attempting deliverance brings shame and regret. Unwillingness to release unnecessary burdens prevents full freedom even when circumstances permit. The superior person must let go of what weighs them down to move freely into new possibility.

### Nine in the Fourth Place

Deliverance of companions brings good fortune; ordinary people cannot achieve this alone. When the superior person liberates others from constraint, alliance and mutual aid result. Acts of liberation create bonds of gratitude and shared movement toward common benefit.

### Six in the Fifth Place

The superior person alone achieves deliverance; inferior persons cannot be liberated through external means. True freedom requires the moral courage and wisdom of the individual; it cannot be granted but must be claimed. This phase brings particular good fortune for those with inner integrity.

### Six at the Top

The duke shoots the hawk perched on the rampart, removing the obstacle that blocks passage to new territory. Final clearing of remaining hindrance through decisive action opens new ground. Complete liberation comes when the last barrier falls away, freeing movement toward genuine advancement.
