# Hexagram 47 — ䷮ 困 (kùn) — Oppression

**Alternate Names:** "Oppression", "Confining", "Exhaustion", "Entrapment"

**Upper Trigram:** ☱ 兌 (duì) — Lake (澤)
**Lower Trigram:** ☵ 坎 (kǎn) — Water (水)

**King Wen Number:** 47 | **Binary:** 011010

---

## Overview

Confining represents hardship and restriction that tests one's character and resilience. Water is confined beneath the lake, its energy trapped and depleted. This hexagram teaches that adversity clarifies values and that maintaining integrity under pressure brings eventual relief.

## Judgment

Oppression teaches that the superior bears hardship with composure and inner certainty, knowing that no condition is permanent. Through persistence in righteousness despite external constraint, one preserves what is essential and eventually finds passage.

## Image

Water trapped below the lake has no outlet; the confined element cannot flow freely. This stagnation represents the limitations that bind action, yet within confinement lies the possibility of inner transformation.

## The Lines

### Six at the Beginning

The first line describes the beginning of oppression when mobility is still restricted but not yet severe. Prompt recognition of constraint and measured response prevent the situation from deepening into greater difficulty.

### Nine in the Second Place

The second line warns that grief and resentment toward oppressive circumstance weaken resolve. Though hardship is real, maintaining composure and even some small joy sustains the spirit through extended confinement.

### Six in the Third Place

The third line speaks of oppression arising from one's own entanglement and binding commitments. The remedy requires honest examination of what one has created; voluntary simplification often brings more relief than waiting for external change.

### Nine in the Fourth Place

The fourth line describes the slow emergence into better circumstance for those of merit. Even in confinement, the worthy are recognized by their equals; help arrives gradually through the loyalty of those who remember their value.

### Nine in the Fifth Place

The fifth line addresses oppression of the superior by the inferior or by circumstance beyond control. Maintaining dignity and avoiding contamination of one's principles, the worthy survive oppression with character intact and reputation preserved.

### Six at the Top

The sixth line warns that remaining confined through one's own inaction or despair prolongs the hardship unnecessarily. The moment of release arrives, but it must be seized; those who have surrendered their will to circumstance may not recognize or act upon liberation.
