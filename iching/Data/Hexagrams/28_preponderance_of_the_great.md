# Hexagram 28 — ䷛ 大過 (dà guò) — Preponderance of the Great

**Alternate Names:** "Preponderance of the Great", "Great Exceeding", "Critical Mass", "Great Surpassing", "Excess"

**Upper Trigram:** ☱ 兌 (duì) — Lake (澤)
**Lower Trigram:** ☴ 巽 (xùn) — Wind (風)

**King Wen Number:** 28 | **Binary:** 011110

---

## Overview

Lake above Wind represents a condition of extremity where normal measures have been exceeded. The yielding element weighs heavily upon the firm, creating an imbalance that cannot endure. This hexagram depicts the crisis point where extraordinary measures become necessary, yet danger accompanies all action.

## Judgment

When the great exceeds its proper measure, collapse threatens. Yet within this extremity lies opportunity for transformation. The superior person acts with utmost caution, using unconventional methods only when necessity demands. Stagnation and action both carry peril in such times.

## Image

Wind bearing a burden too great for its strength suggests the strain at the limits. The image teaches that excess, whether in demands or in responses, strains the structure and cannot be maintained indefinitely.

## The Lines

### Six at the Beginning

The first line, yielding at the base, shows one beginning in an extreme situation. Using reeds as a foundation may seem flimsy, yet this gentle approach prevents further damage. This is the position of wise restraint in crisis.

### Nine in the Second Place

The second line, firm and supporting, indicates one who serves in extremity with capability. Though the burden is great, one's steadiness prevents collapse. This position shows loyalty tested and confirmed.

### Nine in the Third Place

The third line, firm and weakening, depicts one whose strength begins to fail under the load. A loud cry for help is heard but none can answer in time. This position warns of the point past which mere will cannot sustain.

### Nine in the Fourth Place

The fourth line, yielding and rising, shows one who finds unexpected support. Help comes through unusual means and from those below. This position turns the crisis slightly; relief is possible through unconventional alliance.

### Nine in the Fifth Place

The fifth line, yielding in authority, represents a leader whose role is overwhelmed. Too much weight falls upon one person and upon the structure itself. Yet by accepting limitation and calling upon others, order may slowly return.

### Six at the Top

The sixth line, firm at the extremity, shows the point of maximum danger where a step more brings total collapse. The wise do not venture here; the foolish perish. This position teaches that some crests cannot be crossed and some peaks cannot be climbed.
