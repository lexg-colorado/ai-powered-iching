# Hexagram 59 — ䷺ 渙 (huàn) — Dispersion

**Alternate Names:** "Dispersion", "Dispersing", "Dissolution", "Scattering", "Breakthrough"

**Upper Trigram:** ☴ 巽 (xùn) — Wind (風)
**Lower Trigram:** ☵ 坎 (kǎn) — Water (水)

**King Wen Number:** 59 | **Binary:** 110010

---

## Overview

Wind over Water represents the scattering of obstacles and the dissolution of what has become rigid or stale. This hexagram marks a turning point where entanglements unravel and blocked energy flows freely again. Dispersion need not mean loss; it often enables necessary renewal.

## Judgment

When structures become obstacles, their dissolution brings relief and fresh beginning. This time favors releasing old attachments and allowing what no longer serves to scatter naturally. Progress comes through letting go with equanimity rather than clinging desperately.

## Image

Wind scatters what had gathered like clouds dispersing from the sky. The superior person thereby unites the people and directs efforts toward higher principles.

## The Lines

### Six at the Beginning

Early dispersal prevents greater problems from forming. Acting quickly while conditions are fluid prevents hardening into true disaster. Prompt recognition of what must be released opens the way forward.

### Nine in the Second Place

When one person breaks away from consensus, it first creates disorder. Yet this separation serves to clarify true alignment. From dispersion emerges authentic gathering around what truly matters.

### Six in the Third Place

The center offers stability within the scattering process. One's own integrity becomes a anchor others turn toward. In dissolution, inner steadiness becomes a beacon.

### Six in the Fourth Place

Dispersing one's own attachments is more powerful than watching others scatter. When the superior person releases entanglement, others follow naturally into freedom. This voluntary dissolution has great merit.

### Nine in the Fifth Place

Breaking bonds that no longer serve clears the path completely. What has become oppressive must be cast away without regret. Complete separation allows both parties to seek their true alignment.

### Nine at the Top

Extreme dispersal becomes abandonment and loss of foundation. There is a point where letting go becomes irresponsibility. Wisdom knows when to hold what remains valuable and rebuild from dispersed elements.
