# Hexagram 31 — ䷞ 咸 (xián) — Influence

**Alternate Names:** "Influence", "Conjoining", "Wooing", "Mutual Attraction", "Reciprocal Action"

**Upper Trigram:** ☱ 兌 (duì) — Lake (澤)
**Lower Trigram:** ☶ 艮 (gèn) — Mountain (山)

**King Wen Number:** 31 | **Binary:** 011100

---

## Overview

Lake above Mountain represents the yielding touching the still, creating mutual response. This hexagram embodies the principle of influence through attraction rather than force, where the smallest gesture stirs a response in others. Influence moves in both directions; what attracts also binds.

## Judgment

Genuine influence flows naturally when one person truly touches another's heart. The superior person cultivates inner sincerity and receptiveness, knowing that outer action matters less than the truth one embodies. Influence brings good fortune when it arises from inner, not imposed, reasons.

## Image

The youngest daughter and youngest son exchange smiles and glances, each drawn toward the other. The image teaches that influence arises from authentic attraction, not from deliberate strategy or forced manipulation.

## The Lines

### Six at the Beginning

The first line, yielding at the base, shows one whose influence begins in the toes. Movement is initial and tentative; one senses possibility but nothing substantial yet manifests. This is the position of incipient attraction.

### Six in the Second Place

The second line, firm and central, indicates one who influences from a grounded position. The effect is on what is near and what rises toward one. This position brings good fortune through natural gravitation.

### Nine in the Third Place

The third line, yielding and unstable, depicts one whose influence flutters and cannot stabilize. One changes like the wind, unable to commit to any single course. This position brings entanglement and loss.

### Nine in the Fourth Place

The fourth line, firm and approaching authority, shows one whose influence affects those above. Yet the power remains incomplete; one waits for reciprocation. This is the position of the hopeful suitor.

### Nine in the Fifth Place

The fifth line, yielding in authority, represents one who influences from the position of accepting rather than pursuing. By remaining inwardly unmoved, one draws others naturally. This position brings influence through non-action.

### Six at the Top

The sixth line, firm at the summit, shows one whose influence has become complete but also ended. The attraction has served its purpose; stagnation threatens unless a new dynamic begins. This position teaches the impermanence of influence.
