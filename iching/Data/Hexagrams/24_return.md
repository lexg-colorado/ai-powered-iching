# Hexagram 24 — ䷗ 復 (fù) — Return

**Alternate Names:** "Return", "The Turning Point", "Returning", "Restoration"

**Upper Trigram:** ☷ 坤 (kūn) — Earth (地)
**Lower Trigram:** ☳ 震 (zhèn) — Thunder (雷)

**King Wen Number:** 24 | **Binary:** 000001

---

## Overview

Returning signifies the moment when a cycle reaches its nadir and begins its inevitable movement upward. It represents renewal and the restoration of vital energy after a period of depletion or darkness, embodying hope grounded in natural law.

## Judgment

Returning brings great good fortune, particularly in action taken at the critical turning point. Success comes through correct timing and the recognition that this moment of origin permits new beginning without the burdens that accumulated in the previous cycle.

## Image

Thunder emerges from the Earth below, signaling the hidden movement of renewal beneath apparent stillness. The superior person closes themselves within, finding rest and restoration so that energy gathers for the season of growth approaching.

## The Lines

### Nine at the Beginning

At the foundation, the first glimmer of returning appears—subtle, nascent, easily missed. Recognizing and nurturing the smallest sign of renewal requires sensitivity and faith in natural processes.

### Six in the Second Place

Moving toward restoration, one's companions also begin returning to their proper places. Cooperation emerges naturally as the shared movement toward renewal draws allies and supporters together.

### Six in the Third Place

Those who resist the returning current face danger and isolation. Going contrary to the turning point of natural forces brings peril; alignment with the emerging direction ensures safety.

### Six in the Fourth Place

Suspended between the old and new, one may hesitate or proceed with uncertainty. Clear commitment to the returning movement, despite lingering attachment to what has passed, brings breakthrough.

### Six in the Fifth Place

At the fifth line, peaceful return without regret or resistance characterizes the superior person. Having released what had to fall away, the heart returns open and prepared for what comes.

### Six at the Top

At culmination, one risks excess enthusiasm that disrupts the gentle emergence of renewal. The wise person contents themselves with modest progress, knowing that sustainable return requires patience and balanced pacing.
