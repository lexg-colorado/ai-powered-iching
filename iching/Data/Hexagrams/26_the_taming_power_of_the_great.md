# Hexagram 26 — ䷙ 大畜 (dà chù) — The Taming Power of the Great

**Alternate Names:** "The Taming Power of the Great", "Great Accumulation", "Great Storage", "The Great Restraint", "Gathering Strength"

**Upper Trigram:** ☶ 艮 (gèn) — Mountain (山)
**Lower Trigram:** ☰ 乾 (qián) — Heaven (天)

**King Wen Number:** 26 | **Binary:** 100111

---

## Overview

Mountain standing above Heaven represents restraint that subdues even the most powerful forces. This hexagram symbolizes the capacity to hold back and preserve through discipline and proper boundaries. Great power is accumulated not through action but through wise containment and collection.

## Judgment

The superior person, like the mountain, gathers and holds without loss. Through steadfast integrity and correct practice, obstacles transform into resources. What is great can be tamed; what is wild can be gathered when the right methods are employed.

## Image

Heaven beneath the Mountain suggests the accumulation of gifts through boundaries and careful storage. The image teaches that lasting abundance grows from discipline and the proper seasons of rest and gathering.

## The Lines

### Nine at the Beginning

The first line, yielding at the base, indicates the beginning of restraint. One who pulls back from hasty action before acquiring strength prevents danger. This is the position of wise caution in youth.

### Nine in the Second Place

The second line, firm and supporting the structure, represents one who serves the great with capability. Though firm oneself, one yields to higher authority. This position brings no shame through such proper subordination.

### Nine in the Third Place

The third line, firm and at the threshold, shows one whose strength strains against containment. Caution is required here; pressing forward brings harm. The wise recognize when to wait for the proper moment of release.

### Six in the Fourth Place

The fourth line, yielding near the peak, speaks of one who guards the gate of power. Through correct restraint and mediation, one prevents excess and protects what must be preserved. This is the faithful position of the steward.

### Six in the Fifth Place

The fifth line, firm in authority, represents the leader who gathers through inspiration rather than force. Others willingly bring their strengths to such a center of integrity. This position accumulates the greatest treasure.

### Nine at the Top

The sixth line, firm at the summit, shows one whose restraint has become complete mastery. The way opens upward; one ascends to what is pure and unobstructed. This is the culmination of great gathering.
