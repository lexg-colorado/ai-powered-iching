# Hexagram 39 — ䷦ 蹇 (jiǎn) — Obstruction

**Alternate Names:** "Obstruction", "Limping", "Difficulty", "Hardship", "Impediment"

**Upper Trigram:** ☵ 坎 (kǎn) — Water (水)
**Lower Trigram:** ☶ 艮 (gèn) — Mountain (山)

**King Wen Number:** 39 | **Binary:** 010100

---

## Overview

Obstruction represents the genuine difficulty and halted progress that arise from adverse circumstances, yet indicates that difficulty itself is neither permanent nor entirely without benefit. Like a limp that requires careful navigation, obstruction demands adjustment and perseverance. The wisdom lies in recognizing when to advance carefully and when to pause, allowing time for circumstances to shift.

## Judgment

Obstruction is auspicious when the superior person recognizes it and responds with steady persistence rather than forced action. Benefit comes from correct response to difficulty, not from overcoming it by might. The wise adjust their approach and wait for natural openings while maintaining readiness.

## Image

Water piles above mountain, creating blockage and pooling pressure that cannot easily flow downward. This symbolizes the damming up of natural progression and the necessity of finding new channels.

## The Lines

### Six at the Beginning

Coming to obstruction at its beginning allows return before becoming deeply mired. Early recognition of impediment and prompt reversal of course prevents entanglement. The superior person turns back quickly when the way proves blocked.

### Six in the Second Place

The servant cannot reach the sovereign due to intermediaries and obstruction; frustration but no blame. Effort to proceed proves fruitless against actual barriers, yet maintaining respectful patience prevents error. Sometimes persistence must yield to the reality of circumstance.

### Nine in the Third Place

Coming to obstruction and turning back brings good fortune, though inner anguish accompanies the reversal. Recognition that the direct path is closed, and willingness to retreat, preserves ultimate advantage. The emotional difficulty of turning back does not negate the wisdom of doing so.

### Six in the Fourth Place

Coming to obstruction and turning back with companions brings good fortune through mutual support. Shared difficulty borne together strengthens resolve and increases the chance of finding new openings. The presence of trustworthy companions transforms isolated struggle into communal perseverance.

### Nine in the Fifth Place

Great obstruction yields to the correct response and significant persons who can clear the way. When one encounters obstacles of true magnitude, assistance from those with power and wisdom becomes essential. Seeking appropriate help brings success that individual effort cannot achieve.

### Six at the Top

Coming to obstruction and turning back, meeting the king, brings significant good fortune. After extended difficulty and careful withdrawal, alignment with higher authority creates new possibility. This final resolution suggests that the cycle of obstruction eventually yields to renewed progress.
