# Hexagram 48 — ䷯ 井 (jǐng) — The Well

**Alternate Names:** "The Well", "Welling", "The Source"

**Upper Trigram:** ☵ 坎 (kǎn) — Water (水)
**Lower Trigram:** ☴ 巽 (xùn) — Wind (風)

**King Wen Number:** 48 | **Binary:** 010110

---

## Overview

Welling represents the provision of essential sustenance that refreshes and renews all who draw from it. Water rises from deep sources below and flows outward, meeting the needs of the community. This hexagram teaches that true nourishment comes from reliable sources properly accessed.

## Judgment

The Well brings blessing because its gifts are freely drawn by all without depletion. The superior understands that genuine provision is inexhaustible when maintained with care; transformation of self and situation occurs naturally through proper use of what is offered.

## Image

Wind moves across water rising from its source; the well provides without exhaustion. This circulation of natural resource symbolizes sustainable nourishment and the renewal that comes from accessing one's deepest resources.

## The Lines

### Six at the Beginning

The first line describes the well in its unmended state, full of mud and unable to nourish. Before the gifts can flow, the source must be cleansed of obstruction; clearing away what clouds the stream is the necessary first work.

### Nine in the Second Place

The second line speaks of the well that has been abandoned, gathering nothing and offering nothing. Revival requires renewed commitment and care; those who have forgotten to tend the source find it takes time to restore its flow.

### Nine in the Third Place

The third line addresses the well that is clear and ready, yet still unused by the community. Sometimes provision exists but goes unrecognized; those who have prepared their gifts must make their nourishment visible and accessible.

### Six in the Fourth Place

The fourth line emphasizes that maintaining the well with ongoing care ensures constant provision. The one who tends the source with discipline and attention finds their work blessed; others gather without fear and with gratitude.

### Nine in the Fifth Place

The fifth line speaks of the well made accessible and inviting to all without discrimination. The greatest good arises when provision is offered with generosity and without reservation; the giver's fortune grows as more partake of the gift.

### Six at the Top

The sixth line describes the culmination of the well's purpose when it flows abundantly and all are satisfied. At this height, the well itself becomes a center of gathering and transformation; its effects spread far beyond the immediate drawing of water.
