# Hexagram 21 — ䷔ 噬嗑 (shì kè) — Biting Through

**Alternate Names:** "Biting Through", "Gnawing Bite", "Clenching", "Chewing"

**Upper Trigram:** ☲ 離 (lí) — Fire (火)
**Lower Trigram:** ☳ 震 (zhèn) — Thunder (雷)

**King Wen Number:** 21 | **Binary:** 101001

---

## Overview

Biting Through addresses the necessity of decisive action when obstacles block progress. It speaks to the power of concentrated effort and the importance of correct judgment in dealing with resistance or opposition that cannot be avoided or negotiated.

## Judgment

Biting Through brings favorable results when one meets resistance with determined, proportionate force. Success depends on clear understanding of what obstructs progress and on applying sufficient energy precisely at the point of resistance.

## Image

Thunder and Fire together generate heat and penetrating power. The superior person deters wrongdoing through consistent judgment and clear consequences, establishing boundaries that prevent disorder.

## The Lines

### Nine at the Beginning

At the foundation, soft contact with an obstacle precedes forceful action. The beginning of resistance calls for light pressure and testing rather than immediate severity.

### Six in the Second Place

Biting through the initial hardness requires strength and persistence but yields quickly once resistance is overcome. Success in this position comes through decisive engagement.

### Six in the Third Place

At the third position, one encounters tougher resistance demanding greater effort. Continued pressure without fatigue proves necessary, though some damage to one's own capacity occurs.

### Nine in the Fourth Place

Moving toward the center, the obstacle remains substantial and demanding. Focused concentration of energy at the precise weak point allows advancement when brute force alone would fail.

### Six in the Fifth Place

At the fifth line, correct judgment meets with difficulty that requires both force and wisdom. The mature authority applies measured determination, neither harsh nor insufficiently resolute.

### Nine at the Top

At culmination, excessive biting causes injury to oneself. The wise person ceases effort once the true obstacle is removed, avoiding the damage that comes from continuing forcefully when resistance has yielded.
