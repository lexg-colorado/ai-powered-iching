# Hexagram 4 — ䷃ 蒙 (méng) — Youthful Folly

**Alternate Names:** "Youthful Folly", "The Young Shoot", "Discovering", "Ignorance", "The Inexperienced"

**Upper Trigram:** ☶ 艮 (gèn) — Mountain (山)
**Lower Trigram:** ☵ 坎 (kǎn) — Water (水)

**King Wen Number:** 4 | **Binary:** 100010

---

## Overview

Hexagram of youth, ignorance, and the potential for learning. Mountain obscures water below, suggesting limited vision and hidden depths. Represents the innocent unfolding of a new being, the necessity of instruction, and the danger of foolish choices during formative stages.

## Judgment

Youthful Folly finds success through the sage's patient guidance and established teaching. The inexperienced must seek worthy instruction and show receptive obedience. Fortune comes not through the youth's initiative, but through the teacher's steady direction and repeated cycles of learning.

## Image

Mountain standing above spring conveys protection and the gradual emergence of water. The superior person provides clear instruction and repeats teachings to cultivate understanding in the young.

## The Lines

### Six at the Beginning

Shed the shackles of delusion through correction—beneficial to remove fetters. The beginning of ignorance requires firm discipline and removal of false comfort. Genuine learning commences with acknowledgment of error.

### Nine in the Second Place

Encircling the foolish brings favor. The position of patience with the young shows tolerance combined with boundaries. Gentle guidance within clear limits creates the container for growth.

### Six in the Third Place

Take not a maiden in marriage—observe her constancy. Caution against premature binding; discernment reveals who is ready and who remains too unripe. Impatient union brings regret.

### Six in the Fourth Place

Constrained folly brings shame—the youth will not accept guidance. Stubbornness blocks learning; the refusal to follow instruction courts failure. Humility and openness alone permit progress.

### Six in the Fifth Place

Childish folly brings supreme fortune—the mother's devotion. Innocence and genuine receptivity, combined with proper support from authority, yield unexpected benefit. Faith in guidance bears fruit.

### Nine at the Top

To beat the foolish brings misfortune—the way has gone too far. Excessive correction hardens resistance and damages the vessel. Stop before rigidity sets in; permit natural unfoldment within proper bounds.
