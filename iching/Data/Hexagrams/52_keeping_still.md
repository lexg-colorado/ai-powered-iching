# Hexagram 52 — ䷳ 艮 (gèn) — Keeping Still

**Alternate Names:** "Keeping Still", "Bound", "Mountain", "Stillness"

**Upper Trigram:** ☶ 艮 (gèn) — Mountain (山)
**Lower Trigram:** ☶ 艮 (gèn) — Mountain (山)

**King Wen Number:** 52 | **Binary:** 100100

---

## Overview

Keeping Still embodies the power of rest and appropriate cessation. The mountain stands unmoved while all passes beneath and around it. This hexagram teaches that stopping has purpose and that not all progress comes through action.

## Judgment

When the time comes to cease movement, the wise one stops completely and rests in stillness. This is not weakness or defeat but wisdom recognizing that all motion must have season and pause. In absolute stillness, clarity emerges.

## Image

Mountain upon mountain creates immovable presence. The superior person thus knows when to stop and allows others to do likewise, neither rushing nor forcing unnecessary movement.

## The Lines

### Six at the Beginning

The feet are kept still through proper attention to one's place. Begin with the foundation itself, ensuring stability before attempting anything further.

### Six in the Second Place

The legs are held still, unable to follow. When movement is blocked, accept this limitation rather than struggling against it; remaining at rest often brings unexpected benefit.

### Nine in the Third Place

The loins are kept still and the spirit held firm. Control is maintained through inner discipline rather than external force, as the core remains unmoved.

### Six in the Fourth Place

The torso is kept still and movement ceases. The stillness extends inward; when the center is quiet and the will is settled, outer peace follows naturally.

### Six in the Fifth Place

The jaw is kept still and speech ceases. Restraint in expression preserves energy and prevents unnecessary entanglement; the quiet words that come after silence carry authority.

### Nine at the Top

Keeping still with complete presence brings clarity. Having moved through all stages of cessation, one arrives at a state of alert stillness where perception and response arise naturally from rest.
