# Hexagram 7 — ䷆ 師 (shī) — The Army

**Alternate Names:** "The Army", "The Troops", "Multitude", "Leading", "Organization"

**Upper Trigram:** ☷ 坤 (kūn) — Earth (地)
**Lower Trigram:** ☵ 坎 (kǎn) — Water (水)

**King Wen Number:** 7 | **Binary:** 000010

---

## Overview

Hexagram of organized force and disciplined leadership. Water collected beneath the earth suggests hidden power and consolidated strength. Represents the army, legitimate authority in conflict, and the necessity of worthy command to coordinate collective action.

## Judgment

The Army finds supreme success through a strong leader and ordered discipline. Constancy brings blessing; choose leaders of proven competence, not merely the ambitious. The virtue of correct organization permits the multitude to move as one without losing its way.

## Image

Water beneath the earth conveys accumulated force and hidden reserves. The superior person cultivates abundance and gathers the people's reverence, uniting them in common purpose.

## The Lines

### Six at the Beginning

The army sets forth properly ordered—success for those who advance with clear mandate. The beginning demands discipline and clear purpose; those who lack legitimate authority will face failure.

### Nine in the Second Place

Amid the host, the commander receives praise—no blame. The second position of the leader's deputy finds favor through worthy service and clear delegation. Authority properly delegated brings harmony.

### Six in the Third Place

The host perchance carries a corpse—great misfortune. Careless command permits death among the ranks; the loss of order leads to catastrophe. The leader's negligence poisons the entire endeavor.

### Six in the Fourth Place

The army retreats in good order—no blame in the end. Strategic withdrawal from untenable position preserves the force intact. The wise general knows when to advance and when to preserve strength through retreat.

### Six in the Fifth Place

Game hunted in the field—advantageous to be captured. The central position captures the intended prey; legitimate victory comes through right action and proper authority. Success is earned, not stolen.

### Six at the Top

The great lord, having established the kingdom, appoints officers for the provinces—the small man cannot command. The ultimate achievement of the army leader is rightful delegation and the establishment of good governance. Only the worthy ascend to such heights.
