# Hsu Kua — The Sequence of the Hexagrams

**Source:** Wilhelm/Baynes translation, Book III (embedded per hexagram)
**Wing:** 7

---

## The Sequence

### Hexagram 1: Ch'ien / The Creative

*(No sequence entry — first hexagram)*

### Hexagram 2: K'un / The Receptive

*(No sequence entry — first hexagram)*

### Hexagram 3: Chun / Difficulty at the Beginning

After heaven and earth have come into existence, individual beings develop. It is these individual beings that fill the space between heaven and earth. Hence there follows the hexagram of DIFFICULTY AT THE BEGINNING. Difficulty at the beginning is the same as filling up. Chun does not really mean filling up. What is meant is the difficulty that arises when heaven and earth, the light and the shadowy principle, have united for the first time, and all beings are begotten and brought to birth. This produces a chaos that fills up everything, hence the idea of filling up is associated with the hexagram Chun.

### Hexagram 4: Mêng / Youthful Folly

When, after difficulties at the beginning, things have just been born, they are always wrapped at birth in obtuseness. Hence there follows the hexagram of YOUTHFUL FOLLY. For youthful folly means youthful obtuseness. This is the state of things in their youth.

### Hexagram 5: Hsü / Waiting

When things are still small, one must not leave them without nourishment. Hence there follows the hexagram Hsü. Hsü means the way to eating and drinking. The connection between the two meanings of the hexagram—nourishment and waiting—lies in the fact that we must wait to be nourished. Nourishment depends on heaven and the rain. It does not lie within the power of man.

### Hexagram 6: Sung / Conflict

Over meat and drink, there is certain to be conflict. Hence there follows the hexagram of CONFLICT.

### Hexagram 7: Shih / The Army

When there is conflict, the masses are sure to rise up. Hence there follows the hexagram of THE ARMY. Army means mass.

### Hexagram 8: Pi / Holding Together

Among the masses there is surely a reason for uniting. Hence there follows the hexagram of HOLDING TOGETHER. Holding together means uniting.

### Hexagram 9: Hsiao Ch'u / The Taming Power of the Small

Through holding together, restraint is certain to come about. Hence there follows THE TAMING POWER OF THE SMALL.

### Hexagram 10: Lü / Treading

When beings are subjected to restraint the mores arise; hence there follows the hexagram of CONDUCT.

### Hexagram 11: T'ai / Peace

Good conduct, then contentment; thus calm prevails. Hence there follows the hexagram of PEACE. Peace means union, interrelation. The Chinese word t’ai is not easy to translate. It means contentment, rest, peace, in the positive sense of unobstructed, complete union, bringing about a time of flowering and greatness. The movement of the lower trigram Ch’ien tends upward, that of the upper trigram K’un tends downward, and thus they approach each other. This hexagram is correlated with the first month (February–March).

### Hexagram 12: P'i / Standstill

Things cannot remain forever united; hence there follows the hexagram of STANDSTILL. This hexagram is the inverse of the preceding one. Therefore the movements of the trigrams diverge. The trigram Ch’ien above withdraws always farther upward, and K’un below sinks farther and farther down. The two nuclear trigrams, Sun, gentleness, and Kên, Keeping Still, also characterize the hexagram. These trigrams form the hexagram Ku, WORK ON WHAT HAS BEEN SPOILED (18), and in the latter too have the meaning of standstill. The hexagram P’i is linked with the seventh month (August–September).

### Hexagram 13: T'ung Jên / Fellowship with Men

Things cannot be at a standstill forever. Hence there follows the hexagram of FELLOWSIDP WITH MEN.

### Hexagram 14: Ta Yu / Possession in Great Measure

Through FELLOWSHIP WITH MEN things are sure to fall to one’s lot. Hence there follows the hexagram of POSSESSION IN GREAT MEASURE.

### Hexagram 15: Ch'ien / Modesty

He who possesses something great must not make it too full; hence there follows the hexagram of MODESTY.

### Hexagram 16: Yü / Enthusiasm

When one possesses something great and is modest, there is sure to be enthusiasm. Hence there follows the hexagram of ENTHUSIASM.

### Hexagram 17: Sui / Following

Where there is enthusiasm, there is certain to be following. Hence there follows the hexagram of FOLLOWING.

### Hexagram 18: Ku / Work on What Has Been Spoiled

When one follows others with pleasure, there are certain to be undertakings. Hence there follows the hexagram of WORK ON WHAT HAS BEEN SPOILED. Work on what has been spoiled means undertakings.

### Hexagram 19: Lin / Approach

When there are things to do, one can become great. Hence there follows the hexagram of APPROACH. Approach means becoming great.

### Hexagram 20: Kuan / Contemplation

When things are great, one can contemplate them. Hence there follows the hexagram of CONTEMPLATION.

### Hexagram 21: Shih Ho / Biting Through

When there is something that can be contemplated, there is something that creates union. Hence there follows the hexagram of BITING THROUGH. Biting through means union.

### Hexagram 22: Pi / Grace

Things should not unite abruptly and ruthlessly; hence there follows the hexagram of GRACE. Grace is the same as adornment.

### Hexagram 23: Po / Splitting Apart

When one goes too far in adornment, success exhausts itself. Hence there follows the hexagram of SPLITTING APART. Splitting apart means ruin.

### Hexagram 24: Fu / Return

Things cannot be destroyed once and for all. When what is above is completely split apart, it returns below. Hence there follows the hexagram of RETURN.

### Hexagram 25: Wu Wang / Innocence

By turning back one is freed of guilt. Hence there follows the hexagram of INNOCENCE.

### Hexagram 26: Ta Ch'u / The Taming Power of the Great

When innocence is present, it is possible to tame. Hence there follows THE TAMING POWER OF THE GREAT. Holding fast to heavenly virtue is the prerequisite for innocence. On the other hand, innocence is the indispensable condition for being able to hold fast to pristine heavenly virtue.

### Hexagram 27: I / The Corners of the Mouth

When things are held fast, there is provision of nourishment. Hence there follows the hexagram of THE CORNERS OF THE MOUTH. “The corners of the mouth” means the providing of nourishment.

### Hexagram 28: Ta Kuo / Preponderance of the Great

Without provision of nourishment one cannot move; hence there follows the hexagram of PREPONDERANCE OF THE GREAT. Nourishing without putting to use finally evokes movement. Movement without end leads finally too far, to overweighting.

### Hexagram 29: K'an / The Abysmal

Things cannot be permanently in an overweighted state. Hence there follows the hexagram of THE ABYSMAL. The Abysmal means a pit.

### Hexagram 30: Li / The Clinging

In a pit there is certain to be something clinging within. Hence there follows the hexagram of THE CLINGING. The Clinging means resting on something.

### Hexagram 31: Hsien / Influence

After there are heaven and earth, there are the individual things. After individual things have come into being, there are the two sexes. After there are male and female, there is the relationship between husband and wife. After the relationship between husband and wife exists, there is the relationship between father and son. After the relationship between father and son exists, there is the relationship between prince and servitor. After the relationship between prince and servitor exists, there is the difference between superior and inferior. After the difference between superior and inferior exists, the rules of propriety and of right can operate.

### Hexagram 32: Hêng / Duration

The way of husband and wife must not be other than long-lasting. Hence there follows the hexagram of DURATION. Duration means long-lasting.

### Hexagram 33: Tun / Retreat

Things cannot abide forever in their place: hence there follows the hexagram of RETREAT. Retreat means withdrawing.

### Hexagram 34: Ta Chuang / The Power of the Great

Things cannot retreat forever, hence there follows THE POWER OF THE GREAT.

### Hexagram 35: Chin / Progress

Beings cannot stay forever in a state of power; hence there follows the hexagram of PROGRESS. Progress means expansion.

### Hexagram 36: Ming I / Darkening of the Light

Expansion will certainly encounter resistance and injury. Hence there follows the hexagram of DARKENING OF THE LIGHT. Darkening means damage, injury.

### Hexagram 37: Chia Jên / The Family

He who is injured without, of a certainty draws back into his family. Hence there follows the hexagram of THE FAMILY.

### Hexagram 38: K'uei / Opposition

When the way of THE FAMILY draws to an end, misunderstandings come. Hence there follows the hexagram of OPPOSITION. Opposition means misunderstandings.

### Hexagram 39: Chien / Obstruction

Through opposition difficulties necessarily arise. Hence there follows the hexagram of OBSTRUCTION. Obstruction means difficulty.

### Hexagram 40: Hsieh / Deliverance

Things cannot be permanently amid obstructions. Hence there follows the hexagram of DELIVERANCE. Deliverance means release from tension.

### Hexagram 41: Sun / Decrease

Through release of tension something is sure to be lost. Hence there follows the hexagram of DECREASE.

### Hexagram 42: I / Increase

If decrease goes on and on, it is certain to bring about increase. Hence there follows the hexagram of INCREASE.

### Hexagram 43: Kuai / Break-through

If increase goes on unceasingly, there is certain to be a break-through. Hence there follows the hexagram of BREAK-THROUGH. Break-through means resoluteness.

### Hexagram 44: Kou / Coming to Meet

Through resoluteness one is certain to encounter something. Hence there follows the hexagram of COMING TO MEET. Coming to meet means encountering.

### Hexagram 45: Ts'ui / Gathering Together

When creatures meet one another, they mass together. Hence there follows the hexagram of GATHERING TOGETHER. Gathering together means massing.

### Hexagram 46: Shêng / Pushing Upward

Massing toward the top is called pushing upward. Hence there follows the hexagram of PUSHING UPWARD.

### Hexagram 47: K'un / Oppression

If one pushes upward without stopping, he is sure to meet with oppression. Hence there follows the hexagram of OPPRESSION.

### Hexagram 48: Ching / The Well

He who is oppressed above is sure to turn downward. Hence there follows the hexagram of THE WELL.

### Hexagram 49: Ko / Revolution

The setup of a well must necessarily be revolutionized in the course of time. Hence there follows the hexagram of REVOLUTION. A well must be cleaned out from time to time or it will become clogged with mud. Therefore the hexagram Ching, THE WELL, which means a permanent setup, is followed by the hexagram of REVOLUTION, showing the need of changes in long-established institutions, in order to keep them from stagnating.

### Hexagram 50: Ting / The Caldron

Nothing transforms things so much as the ting. Hence there follows the hexagram of THE CALDRON. The transformations wrought by Ting are on the one hand the changes produced in food by cooking, and on the other, in a figurative sense, the revolutionary effects resulting from the joint work of a prince and a sage.

### Hexagram 51: Chên / The Arousing

Among the custodians of the sacred vessels, the eldest son stands first. Hence there follows the hexagram of THE AROUSING. The Arousing means movement.

### Hexagram 52: Kên / Keeping Still

Things cannot move continuously, one must make them stop. Hence there follows the hexagram of KEEPING STILL. Keeping Still means stopping.

### Hexagram 53: Chien / Development

Things cannot stop forever; hence there follows the hexagram of DEVELOPMENT. Development means to progress.

### Hexagram 54: Kuei Mei / The Marrying Maiden

Through progress one is sure to reach the place where one belongs. Hence there follows the hexagram of THE MARRYING MAIDEN.1

### Hexagram 55: Fêng / Abundance

That which attains a place in which it is at home is sure to become great. Hence there follows the hexagram of ABUNDANCE. Abundance means greatness.

### Hexagram 56: Lü / The Wanderer

Whatever greatness may exhaust itself upon, this much is certain: it loses its home. Hence there follows the hexagram of THE WANDERER.

### Hexagram 57: Sun / The Gentle

The wanderer has nothing that might receive him; hence there follows the hexagram of THE GENTLE, THE PENETRATING. The Gentle means going into. This means that the wanderer in his forlornness has no place to stay in, and that hence there follows Sun, the hexagram of homecoming.

### Hexagram 58: Tui / The Joyous

When one has penetrated something, one rejoices. Hence there follows the hexagram of THE JOYOUS. The Joyous means to rejoice.

### Hexagram 59: Huan / Dispersion

After joy comes dispersal. Hence there follows the hexagram of DISPERSION. Dispersion means scattering.

### Hexagram 60: Chieh / Limitation

Things cannot be forever separate. Hence there follows the hexagram of LIMITATION.

### Hexagram 61: Chung Fu / Inner Truth

Through being limited, things are made dependable. Hence there follows the hexagram of INNER TRUTH.

### Hexagram 62: Hsiao Kuo / Preponderance of the Small

When one has the trust of creatures, one sets them in motion; hence there follows the hexagram of PREPONDERANCE OF THE SMALL.

### Hexagram 63: Chi Chi / After Completion

He who stands above things brings them to completion. Hence there follows the hexagram of AFTER COMPLETION.

### Hexagram 64: Wei Chi / Before Completion

Things cannot exhaust themselves. Hence there follows, at the end, the hexagram of BEFORE COMPLETION.
