# Hexagram 15 — ䷎ 謙 (qiān) — Modesty

**Alternate Names:** "Modesty", "Humbling", "Humility", "Moderation", "Self-Effacement"

**Upper Trigram:** ☷ 坤 (kūn) — Earth (地)
**Lower Trigram:** ☶ 艮 (gèn) — Mountain (山)

**King Wen Number:** 15 | **Binary:** 000100

---

## Overview

Humbling represents the power of quiet modesty and the wisdom of not displaying capability. Like a mountain beneath the earth, this hexagram shows strength concealed within lowliness. True greatness operates without announcing itself; virtue speaks more effectively through humble bearing.

## Judgment

Humility brings fortune through removing obstacles created by pride and self-assertion. The modest person advances without exciting resentment; genuine quality needs no advertisement to be recognized by the wise. Grace flows from those who make space for others to shine.

## Image

The mountain sinking into the earth represents strength held in reserve, power manifested as steadiness rather than display. Modesty here is not weakness but supreme confidence so complete it requires no assertion.

## The Lines

### Six at the Beginning

The first line embodies fundamental modesty without affectation, being naturally humble. Simple unpretentious action meets no resistance; others respond to genuine sincerity with willing cooperation and support.

### Six in the Second Place

The second line balances humility with inner certainty, modest in appearance yet firm in substance. This combination of external restraint and internal strength allows wise advancement; reputation grows through quiet consistent virtue.

### Nine in the Third Place

The third line approaches the threshold where modesty might become excessive or be mistaken for weakness. Discernment becomes necessary to maintain appropriate modest demeanor while ensuring genuine contribution is recognized; true service requires visible result.

### Six in the Fourth Place

The fourth line rises toward position of greater influence while maintaining humble bearing. This paradox of power held lightly attracts genuine allegiance; others serve willingly when leadership asks nothing but best effort toward worthy purpose.

### Six in the Fifth Place

The fifth line holds central position with natural authority flowing from demonstrated virtue. The humble ruler accomplishes great works without claiming credit; blessing flows through such a person to all below, and loyalty is freely given.

### Six at the Top

The sixth line shows excessive application of modesty, where self-effacement becomes counterproductive. The time comes when hidden light must shine, when virtue must be visible; retreating too far from effectiveness ultimately serves no one.
