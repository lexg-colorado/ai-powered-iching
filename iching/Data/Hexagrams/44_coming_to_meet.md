# Hexagram 44 — ䷫ 姤 (gòu) — Coming to Meet

**Alternate Names:** "Coming to Meet", "Coupling", "Encountering", "Temptation"

**Upper Trigram:** ☰ 乾 (qián) — Heaven (天)
**Lower Trigram:** ☴ 巽 (xùn) — Wind (風)

**King Wen Number:** 44 | **Binary:** 111110

---

## Overview

Coupling represents an unexpected encounter that requires discernment and proper response. From the vast expanse of heaven comes the subtle beginning of wind, meeting what rises from below. This hexagram teaches vigilance against seduction by the inferior.

## Judgment

Coming to Meet warns that the moment of first contact determines all that follows. The superior must meet what is inferior with clarity and firmness; indulgence at the threshold allows corruption to take root.

## Image

Heaven's vast space is penetrated by the first stirrings of wind. This initial invasion of the empty by the subtle suggests how small compromises accumulate into larger domination.

## The Lines

### Six at the Beginning

The first line advises restraint when temptation first appears, before it takes hold. A firm boundary established at the outset prevents the elaborate entanglement that would require painful separation later.

### Nine in the Second Place

The second line describes being bound to duty and unable to succumb to distraction. Those with clear responsibilities find protection in their obligations; the smaller path of service shields against seduction.

### Nine in the Third Place

The third line warns that indulgence with what is corrupt brings persistent harm. Once intimate contact is established, the entanglement becomes difficult to dissolve, and residual contamination lingers.

### Nine in the Fourth Place

The fourth line speaks of meeting what is troublesome with clarity and without deception. When one faces difficulty openly and honestly, acknowledging both attraction and wisdom, clean parting becomes possible.

### Nine in the Fifth Place

The fifth line emphasizes that the superior maintains proper distance while dealing with the inferior. Benevolence need not mean familiarity; one can acknowledge and address without allowing infiltration.

### Nine at the Top

The sixth line describes the culmination of improper encounter, where continued denial leads to isolation and regret. The moment of reckoning arrives when compromise has advanced too far; acceptance of what has been lost becomes necessary.
