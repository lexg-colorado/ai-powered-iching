# Hexagram 58 — ䷹ 兌 (duì) — The Joyous

**Alternate Names:** "The Joyous", "Open", "Lake", "Pleasure", "Enjoyment"

**Upper Trigram:** ☱ 兌 (duì) — Lake (澤)
**Lower Trigram:** ☱ 兌 (duì) — Lake (澤)

**King Wen Number:** 58 | **Binary:** 011011

---

## Overview

The doubled Lake trigram embodies joyousness and openness, expressing the principle of satisfaction and mutual delight. This hexagram represents the power of genuine joy to attract and inspire others. The openness allows free exchange and creates conditions for harmony.

## Judgment

Joy spreads when it arises from genuine inner contentment and authentic expression. This hexagram favors undertakings that bring people together in shared pleasure and honest communication. Progress flows from creating an environment where others feel welcomed and valued.

## Image

Two lakes connected allow free exchange and reflection. The superior person uses righteous joy to inspire and unite people in common endeavor.

## The Lines

### Nine at the Beginning

Joy begins with simplicity and natural contentment. One finds satisfaction in small things without grasping for more. This foundation supports genuine pleasure that invites no envy or complication.

### Nine in the Second Place

When joy remains honest and free from pretense, it deepens naturally. Trust and sincerity draw trustworthy companions. Simple pleasures shared are more enduring than elaborate ones seized.

### Six in the Third Place

Excessive joy becomes intoxication and loss of judgment. Indulgence clouds clarity and invites misfortune. The superior person restrains enjoyment before it becomes destructive.

### Nine in the Fourth Place

With joy as the guiding principle, one weighs options carefully. Not all pleasures serve growth; discrimination is required. True satisfaction comes from choosing what truly benefits all.

### Nine in the Fifth Place

Joy and trust intermingle, creating openness to deception. Caution must temper openness; not everyone deserves full confidence. Joyous engagement need not mean abandoning discernment.

### Six at the Top

Joy pursued excessively becomes hollow and leads astray. The time demands releasing attachment to pleasure and returning to steadier ground. Growth comes from knowing when to step back from revelry.
