# Hexagram 18 — ䷑ 蠱 (gǔ) — Work on What Has Been Spoiled

**Alternate Names:** "Work on What Has Been Spoiled", "Decay", "Correcting", "Rectification"

**Upper Trigram:** ☶ 艮 (gèn) — Mountain (山)
**Lower Trigram:** ☴ 巽 (xùn) — Wind (風)

**King Wen Number:** 18 | **Binary:** 100110

---

## Overview

Correcting addresses situations where decay, neglect, or mismanagement have created disorder. It calls for decisive intervention and patient reconstruction, acknowledging that corruption did not arise suddenly and therefore demands sustained effort to remedy.

## Judgment

Correcting brings advantage when undertaken with clear resolve and appropriate timing. Success requires identifying the root causes of decay and systematically addressing them through disciplined action and sustained commitment.

## Image

Wind penetrating the depths of the Mountain reveals hidden corruption in the foundations. The superior person brings order and renewal through careful examination and deliberate reconstruction.

## The Lines

### Six at the Beginning

At the foundation, one encounters the direct consequences of inherited spoilage or ancestral error. Accepting responsibility for restoration, though not one's making, opens the path to genuine correction.

### Nine in the Second Place

Moderate correction applied with patience and skill restores function without excessive force. Avoid the temptation to blame those who created the disorder; focus instead on systematic renewal.

### Nine in the Third Place

Applying correction beyond necessity risks creating new imbalances. Prudent action involves modest measures taken consistently rather than dramatic gestures of reform.

### Six in the Fourth Place

Tolerance of minor imperfection may be appropriate during reconstruction. Perfectionism at this stage exhausts energy needed for larger structural repairs.

### Six in the Fifth Place

Inherited obligations demand attention and correction with filial respect. Working through corruption within family or established institutions requires both rigor and reverence.

### Nine at the Top

At culmination, successful correction produces not merely the absence of decay but genuine renewal of vital function. The corrector gains respect through demonstrated competence and patience.
