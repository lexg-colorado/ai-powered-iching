# Hexagram 41 — ䷨ 損 (sǔn) — Decrease

**Alternate Names:** "Decrease", "Diminishing", "Loss", "Sacrifice"

**Upper Trigram:** ☶ 艮 (gèn) — Mountain (山)
**Lower Trigram:** ☱ 兌 (duì) — Lake (澤)

**King Wen Number:** 41 | **Binary:** 100011

---

## Overview

Diminishing represents the necessity of reduction and voluntary sacrifice for spiritual progress. When excess is removed and attachments are released, clarity and balance emerge. This hexagram counsels the withdrawal of superficial elements to strengthen the essential core.

## Judgment

Diminishing teaches that strategic reduction brings good fortune when pursued with proper timing and sincerity. The sage reduces what is excessive in order to nourish what is vital, understanding that loss of the insignificant leads to gain of the fundamental.

## Image

Below the mountain lies the lake, its waters naturally flowing downward and away. This descent of energy symbolizes the voluntary diminishment of the outer to fortify the inner.

## The Lines

### Nine at the Beginning

The first line advises hastening to complete small duties without hesitation. When a task is small and necessary, delay becomes a greater fault than swift action, even if imperfect.

### Nine in the Second Place

The second line warns against excessive withdrawal or self-diminishment. One must reduce external entanglements but not abandon one's own integrity and essential nature.

### Six in the Third Place

The third line speaks of the benefit when three walk together and one departs. Not all associations serve growth; parting ways with incompatible elements strengthens the remaining bond.

### Six in the Fourth Place

The fourth line addresses diminishing one's illnesses and sorrows through proper action. When faults are reduced through effort and awareness, fortune naturally returns.

### Six in the Fifth Place

The fifth line emphasizes supreme good fortune when one increases others while asking nothing in return. The greatest diminishment of self-interest paradoxically brings the highest blessing.

### Nine at the Top

The sixth line warns that excessive pushing forward without releasing the past leads to harm. True completion requires both action and non-action, both gain and loss, in their proper season.
