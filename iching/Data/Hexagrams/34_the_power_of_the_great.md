# Hexagram 34 — ䷡ 大壯 (dà zhuàng) — The Power of the Great

**Alternate Names:** "The Power of the Great", "Great Strength", "Great Invigorating", "Great Maturity", "Great Force"

**Upper Trigram:** ☳ 震 (zhèn) — Thunder (雷)
**Lower Trigram:** ☰ 乾 (qián) — Heaven (天)

**King Wen Number:** 34 | **Binary:** 001111

---

## Overview

Great Invigorating represents the accumulation of power and vitality in proper alignment. Yang force ascends strongly under proper governance, creating dynamic action tempered by righteousness. This is the time when great strength flows freely, yet must be guided by moral direction to avoid excess.

## Judgment

Great Invigorating is auspicious when power is applied with integrity. The strength available now can accomplish significant works, yet caution against willful arrogance is essential. Rectitude in the exercise of power ensures that growth becomes lasting and beneficial.

## Image

Thunder moves above Heaven, intensifying celestial power and creating vibrant forward momentum. This symbolizes mighty yang force advancing with the clarity of Heaven's principles guiding the way.

## The Lines

### Nine at the Beginning

Strength in the feet moves cautiously into new territory. Premature assertion of power brings obstruction and misstep. The beginning phase of invigoration requires restraint and testing of ground before full commitment.

### Nine in the Second Place

Strength held firmly through persistence brings good fortune without error. When internal strength is solid and stable, one need not rush. Steady advancement proves superior to hurried assertions of power.

### Nine in the Third Place

The inferior person relies on strength of body alone; the superior person combines strength with virtue. Without righteousness, even great power becomes destructive. The integration of moral principle with vigor marks true invigoration.

### Nine in the Fourth Place

Vigorous restraint prevents the fence of petulance from breaking uselessly. Strength must sometimes hold back from striking; knowing when to restrain mighty force demonstrates superior judgment. Patience in power prevents senseless damage.

### Six in the Fifth Place

Strength loses its way when divorced from cautious wisdom. The loss of a goat through careless exertion symbolizes small misfortune from uncontrolled assertion. Yet no great harm results, as the superior quickly perceives the error.

### Six at the Top

Strength forced against immovable resistance leads to entanglement and fatigue. The goat with horns locked cannot retreat nor advance. Recognizing when power cannot overcome, and withdrawing with dignity, prevents ultimate disaster.
