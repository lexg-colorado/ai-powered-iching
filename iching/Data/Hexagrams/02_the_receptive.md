# Hexagram 2 — ䷁ 坤 (kūn) — The Receptive

**Alternate Names:** "The Receptive", "Acquiescence", "The Flow", "Pure Yin", "The Mare"

**Upper Trigram:** ☷ 坤 (kūn) — Earth (地)
**Lower Trigram:** ☷ 坤 (kūn) — Earth (地)

**King Wen Number:** 2 | **Binary:** 000000

---

## Overview

Hexagram of pure receptive power and nurturing support. Embodies the complementary principle of earth, expressing ultimate yin energy in service and yielding strength. Represents responsiveness, devotion, and the sustaining capacity of all creation.

## Judgment

The Receptive brings success through square accomplishment and persistence. Serve with steadfast dedication and unwavering loyalty, following established patterns and the guidance of the superior. The way lies in adaptation and boundless capacity to contain.

## Image

Earth bearing all things above earth conveys boundless support and nurturing embrace. The superior person cultivates magnanimous character and develops carrying capacity to support all beings.

## The Lines

### Six at the Beginning

Hoarfrost underfoot—solid ice will follow. The first sign of receiving principle warns of inevitable progression toward crystallization. Begin with caution and appropriate response to subtle movements.

### Six in the Second Place

The straight, broad, advantageous in all matters. Perfect alignment with receiving principle ensures success through directness and unobstructed capacity. This is the inner realm of loyal service.

### Six in the Third Place

Hiding the gifts—beneficial to sustain the inner. Though capable, one preserves strength and conceals ability, working without display. This threshold position counsels restraint before fuller engagement.

### Six in the Fourth Place

Bound in the sack—no blame, no praise, nothing ventured. Restraint and careful closure protect against premature action or exposure. A time for silence and protected inner work.

### Six in the Fifth Place

Yellow undergarment brings supreme fortune. Centered, moderate, and naturally beautiful—the perfect expression of receptive principle. Authority rests upon humble, natural virtue.

### Six at the Top

Dragons battle in the meadow—blood flows black and yellow. The receptive, pressed to its extreme, becomes rigid and combative. Conflict arises when yin oversteps its nature; retreat into proper bounds prevents ruin.
