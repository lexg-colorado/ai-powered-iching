# Hexagram 27 — ䷚ 頤 (yí) — The Corners of the Mouth

**Alternate Names:** "The Corners of the Mouth", "Nourishment", "Jaws", "Swallowing", "Provisions"

**Upper Trigram:** ☶ 艮 (gèn) — Mountain (山)
**Lower Trigram:** ☳ 震 (zhèn) — Thunder (雷)

**King Wen Number:** 27 | **Binary:** 100001

---

## Overview

Mountain over Thunder forms the image of the mouth, opened and closed. This hexagram concerns nourishment in the broadest sense—what enters and sustains life, whether physical food or spiritual teaching. Proper nourishment requires both discrimination in what is taken in and correctness in how it is given.

## Judgment

Attention to nourishment in all its forms determines health and growth. The superior person watches carefully what goes into the mouth and labors to provide proper sustenance for others. Fortune depends on correct selection and proper restraint in consumption.

## Image

The mouth remains at rest except when truly needed for nourishment and speech. The image teaches that words and food are not frivolously consumed; both require purpose and measure.

## The Lines

### Nine at the Beginning

The first line, yielding at the base, shows one who abandons their own strength to follow another. Though this brings loss, if done with correct purpose it removes obstacles to advancement. This is the position of humble reception.

### Six in the Second Place

The second line, firm and central, indicates one who seeks nourishment from superior sources. When one has means yet turns to others for sustenance, envy arises. The wise provide for themselves and do not depend on unequal exchange.

### Six in the Third Place

The third line, yielding and unbalanced, warns against seeking nourishment through incorrect means. Greed and improper desire lead to entanglement and loss. This position shows that wrong methods of obtaining sustenance bring misfortune.

### Six in the Fourth Place

The fourth line, firm and approaching authority, depicts one whose nourishment comes from those above. Moving against this current brings difficulty; yielding to receive brings good fortune. This is the position of the sustained student.

### Six in the Fifth Place

The fifth line, yielding in authority, shows one who cannot provide nourishment and should not attempt it. By departing from the proper role and acknowledging limitation, one avoids disaster. Knowing what one cannot do is wisdom.

### Nine at the Top

The sixth line, firm at the summit, represents the sage who provides nourishment to all. Through selfless giving and correct teaching, lasting benefit spreads far. This is the position of the great nourisher.
